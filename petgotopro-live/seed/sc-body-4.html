👩‍🍳 5 Fun Recipes Using Stella & Chewy's

Every one of these uses a product from the range as the flavour engine, which means you're adding real meat nutrition rather than empty biscuit. All five are simple enough to make with children helping.

⚠️ Read this before you bake anything for a dog

Never use: xylitol / birch sugar (check every peanut butter label — it's lethal to dogs), chocolate or cocoa, raisins, grapes or currants, macadamia nuts, onion, garlic, leek or chive in any form, nutmeg, alcohol, or anything with added salt or sugar.

Portion rule: treats should be no more than 10% of your dog's daily calories — and homemade treats count. Reduce dinner slightly on baking day. Introduce any new ingredient in a small amount first, and skip these entirely for dogs with pancreatitis, diabetes or diagnosed food allergies unless your vet approves.

🧊 1. Bone Broth Pupsicles

Makes: 1 ice cube trayTime: 5 min + freezingDifficulty: Very easyKeeps: 2 months frozen

You need: Bountiful Bone Broth · water · a few blueberries or small blackberries (optional)

Method: Mix the bone broth half-and-half with water — this dilutes the calories and makes the cubes less rich. Drop a berry into each compartment of an ice cube tray, pour the mixture over, and freeze for four hours.

Why it's good: the single best hot-weather treat there is. It cools, it hydrates, and it takes a dog several minutes of licking to finish — which is genuinely calming. Perfect for teething puppies and for older dogs who don't drink enough.

🍪 2. Meal Mixer Training Cookies

Makes: ~40 small cookiesTime: 30 minDifficulty: EasyKeeps: 1 week sealed, 3 months frozen

You need: 1 cup oat flour (blitz porridge oats in a blender) · ½ cup plain pumpkin purée (not pie filling) · 1 egg · ¼ cup Meal Mixers, crushed to crumbs

Method: Heat the oven to 350°F / 175°C. Crush the meal mixers in a bag with a rolling pin. Mix everything into a stiff dough, adding a splash of water if it's too dry. Roll to about ¼ inch thick, cut into small squares or use a cutter, and bake 20–25 minutes until firm and dry. Cool completely before storing.

Why it's good: cut them small and you have high-value training treats that cost a fraction of shop-bought ones — and unlike most biscuits, the flavour comes from real meat, organ and bone rather than flavouring.

🥜 3. No-Bake Dinner Dust Bites

Makes: ~20 bitesTime: 10 min + chillingDifficulty: No oven neededKeeps: 1 week refrigerated

You need: ½ cup xylitol-free peanut butter · ¾ cup oat flour · 2 tbsp Marie's Magical Dinner Dust · 1 tbsp water if needed

Method: Mix the peanut butter and oat flour into a thick dough. Roll into small balls, then roll each ball in dinner dust to coat the outside — the coating is where the aroma comes from and it's what makes dogs go slightly mad for these. Chill for 30 minutes to firm up.

Why it's good: no oven, takes ten minutes, and the meaty outer coating makes them irresistible for recall training. Check the peanut butter label for xylitol every single time — brands reformulate.

🦴 4. Frozen Enrichment Kong

Makes: 1 stuffed KongTime: 5 min + freezingDifficulty: Very easyKeeps: 1 month frozen

You need: Bone broth · 2 tbsp plain unsweetened yoghurt (skip if your dog is dairy-sensitive) · a handful of Meal Mixers · a rubber stuffable toy

Method: Plug the small hole of the Kong with a smear of peanut butter. Drop in the meal mixers, spoon in the yoghurt, then top up with bone broth. Stand it upright in a mug and freeze overnight.

Why it's good: this is the single most useful thing on this list. A frozen stuffed Kong takes 20–45 minutes to work through, and that licking and chewing is genuinely calming — it's a standard recommendation for separation anxiety, crate training and settling a dog when visitors arrive. Make a batch and keep them in the freezer.

🎂 5. Birthday Pupcakes

Makes: 6 cupcakesTime: 35 minDifficulty: EasyKeeps: 3 days refrigerated

You need: 1 cup oat flour · ½ cup cooked mashed sweet potato · 1 egg · 2 tbsp unsweetened apple sauce · 1 tsp baking powder. For the topping: plain unsweetened yoghurt or a little mashed sweet potato, plus Dinner Dust to sprinkle.

Method: Oven to 350°F / 175°C. Mix the cake ingredients, spoon into a lined muffin tin (fill about two-thirds), bake 20–25 minutes until a skewer comes out clean. Cool completely — this matters, hot topping slides straight off. Swirl on the yoghurt and finish with a generous dusting of Dinner Dust instead of sprinkles.

Why it's good: a proper celebration cake with no sugar, no chocolate and no salt. One pupcake is plenty for a medium dog on the day — freeze the rest, or share them with the dogs at a birthday party.

💚 Two bonus tricks that need no recipe at all

The crumble topper: crush a freeze-dried patty over your dog's normal food and add a tablespoon of warm water. Thirty seconds of effort transforms a boring bowl, and it's the fastest way to get a fussy dog eating again.

The pill wrapper: rehydrate a small piece of a freeze-dried patty into a paste and mould it around a tablet. It works on dogs that have learned to spot cheese and hot dogs, because the smell is far stronger.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is freeze-dried raw safer than frozen raw?

Generally yes, for two reasons: Stella & Chewy's use high-pressure processing to eliminate pathogens without cooking, and freeze-drying removes the water that bacteria need to multiply. The FDA found meaningfully high contamination rates in raw pet food overall — around 7.7% Salmonella and 16.3% Listeria in their sampling — so HPP plus batch testing is a real advantage. Still handle it like raw meat: wash hands, disinfect bowls, don't let your dog lick your face afterwards.

Do I have to rehydrate the freeze-dried patties?

It's recommended and we'd do it. Adding warm water improves digestibility, releases the aroma that makes dogs enthusiastic, and adds moisture — which is one of the main benefits of feeding this way. Some dogs happily eat them dry as a crunchy treat, which is fine occasionally, but make sure fresh water is always available.

Is grain-free food safe for my dog?

The FDA has investigated a possible link between grain-free, legume-heavy diets and dilated cardiomyopathy. It isn't fully resolved. Stella & Chewy's grain-free recipes are marketed as pea-free, lentil-free and potato-free, which addresses the ingredients under scrutiny, and they also offer Wholesome Grains and SuperBlends lines. If your dog is a breed predisposed to DCM, ask your vet which line they'd recommend.

Can I mix it with my current kibble?

Yes, and for most households that's the smartest approach — meal mixers or dinner dust over a good kibble gives you most of the benefit at a much lower cost. Just remember toppers add calories: reduce the kibble portion accordingly, or you'll gradually put weight on your dog.

Is it suitable for puppies?

They make Perfectly Puppy formulas in both patties and mixers, formulated for growth with DHA for brain and eye development. Large and giant-breed puppies are the exception worth flagging — their calcium and phosphorus intake needs careful control during growth to protect developing joints, so check with your vet before choosing any food for a large-breed puppy.

How long do the treats I make at home keep?

Baked cookies last about a week in an airtight container at room temperature, or three months frozen. No-bake bites need refrigerating and last about a week. Frozen pupsicles and stuffed Kongs keep one to two months. Because there are no preservatives, don't leave homemade treats out in a bowl — bake small batches and freeze the rest.

The Bottom Line

Stella & Chewy's earns its reputation. The founding story is real, the high-pressure processing they adopted back in 2007 addresses the single biggest legitimate objection to raw feeding, the meat inclusion is genuinely high, and offering both grain-free and wholesome grains lines is a sensible response to an unresolved question rather than a marketing dodge.

The honest caveats are cost, the fact that raw handling hygiene still matters even with HPP, and that no food is right for every dog — particularly those with existing health conditions or large-breed puppies.

If you're curious but not ready to spend $40 a bag, start with a 1 oz bag of Meal Mixers or a pot of Dinner Dust over the food you already buy. You'll know within a week whether your dog is interested, and you'll have spent very little finding out. Then go and make them a batch of bone broth pupsicles, because that's the genuinely fun part.
