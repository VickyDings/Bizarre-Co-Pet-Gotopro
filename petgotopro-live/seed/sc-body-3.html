💪 Why This Kind of Food Benefits a Dog

Setting the brand aside for a moment — here's what a high-meat, minimally processed, moisture-rich diet actually does, and why owners notice the difference.

Better digestibility means more nutrition absorbed

Highly digestible protein means a greater share of what goes in is actually used rather than passed through. The most obvious everyday evidence is smaller, firmer, less smelly stools — unglamorous, but it's the clearest signal you have that absorption has improved. Most owners notice this within one to two weeks.

Skin and coat

Coat quality is one of the first visible things to change on a higher-quality diet, usually over four to eight weeks as new hair grows in. Omega-3 fatty acids (particularly from salmon and sardines), zinc, biotin and quality protein are the drivers. If your dog is itchy, though, a food change is a starting point rather than a diagnosis — allergies, parasites and skin infections all need veterinary investigation.

Moisture, and why it matters more than people think

Dry kibble contains around 10% moisture; wet food and rehydrated raw are closer to 70%. Chronic mild dehydration is genuinely common in dogs fed dry food who don't drink much, and it puts steady, low-level strain on the kidneys and urinary tract over a lifetime. This is the same principle behind everything we write about cats and hydration — water taken in with food is the easiest water to get into an animal.

Rehydrating freeze-dried patties with warm water, adding Shredrs, or pouring bone broth over kibble all achieve this. Combine it with genuinely clean, filtered water in the bowl and you've addressed one of the most under-appreciated aspects of long-term health.

Energy, weight and muscle

High protein with a controlled calorie load supports lean muscle mass — which matters especially for seniors, who lose muscle steadily with age. Owners frequently report more consistent energy rather than the peaks and crashes of a very carbohydrate-heavy diet.

Dental benefit — real, but limited

Chewing firmer foods and dental chews does help reduce plaque. It does not replace tooth brushing or professional cleaning, and any brand claiming otherwise is overselling. Dental disease is one of the most common health problems in dogs, and diet alone won't prevent it.

⚖️ The Honest Drawbacks

No review is worth reading if it only lists positives. Here's what to weigh up.

⚠️ 1. Raw food safety — the part most brand reviews skip

The FDA's position on raw pet food is cautious for good reason. In a two-year study of 196 raw pet food samples, they found Salmonella in around 7.7% and Listeria monocytogenes in around 16.3% — dramatically higher contamination than dry food, semi-moist food or jerky treats, which showed little or none.

Where Stella & Chewy's differs: they've used high-pressure processing since 2007 specifically to eliminate pathogens without cooking, and state that every batch is tested before release. That is a materially better risk profile than untreated frozen raw. But "much lower risk" is not "no risk."

Handle it like raw meat regardless: wash hands for 20 seconds afterwards, disinfect bowls and surfaces, don't let your dog lick your face after eating, and don't leave food sitting out. The FDA is most concerned about households with infants, elderly people, pregnant women or anyone immunocompromised — if that's your home, discuss raw feeding with your vet before starting.

⚠️ 2. Grain-free and heart disease

The FDA has investigated a possible link between grain-free, legume-heavy diets and dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) in dogs. The picture is still not fully resolved and no single cause has been confirmed, but it's a live question worth being aware of.

Two things are relevant here. Their grain-free recipes are marketed as pea-free, lentil-free and potato-free, which addresses the specific ingredients under scrutiny. And they now offer Wholesome Grains and SuperBlends lines for owners who'd rather avoid the question entirely. If your dog is a breed predisposed to DCM — Dobermans, Great Danes, Boxers, Cocker Spaniels and Golden Retrievers among them — talk to your vet about which line suits.

⚠️ 3. Cost

This is genuinely expensive food. Feeding freeze-dried raw as a sole diet to a large dog runs into serious money, and the 21 lb kibble bags sit at $80–$97. For most households the sensible answer is a hybrid: a good kibble as the base with meal mixers, dinner dust or bone broth on top. You capture most of the benefit at a fraction of the cost.

⚠️ 4. It's rich — transition slowly

Moving a dog straight from a standard kibble to high-meat raw commonly causes loose stools, simply because the digestive system hasn't adjusted. This is not the food "not agreeing" with your dog; it's a transition problem, and it's avoidable. See the schedule below.

⚠️ 5. Not right for every dog

Dogs with pancreatitis or a history of it, dogs with certain kidney or liver conditions, and immunosuppressed dogs (including those on chemotherapy or long-term steroids) may need a different approach. Large-breed puppies need carefully controlled calcium during growth. Any dog with a diagnosed condition should have a diet change discussed with their vet first — that's not a disclaimer, it's genuinely important.

💚 Transition tips that actually help

Go slower for sensitive dogs — 14 days rather than 10 is fine. Rehydrate freeze-dried food with warm (not hot) water and let it stand a minute; it improves both digestibility and aroma. Keep a plain pumpkin purée in the cupboard for loose stools. And change one thing at a time — if you switch food and add a new supplement in the same week, you'll never know which caused what.
