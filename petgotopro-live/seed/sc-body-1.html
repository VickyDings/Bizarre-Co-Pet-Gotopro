In 1997, a woman in Brooklyn walked into an animal shelter and was told not to adopt one of the dogs. He was too sick, the vet said — an autoimmune disease, a poor prognosis, not a sensible choice. She adopted him anyway. His name was Chewy.

That decision is why this brand exists. Marie Moody's vet suggested trying a raw diet of protein, fruit and vegetables, so she started making Chewy's meals herself in her brownstone kitchen. His health turned around dramatically. By 2003 she had turned it into a company, named for Chewy and her other rescue dog, Stella — and today Stella & Chewy's runs a 355,000 square foot facility in Oak Creek, Wisconsin, a long way from that first kitchen.

It's a genuinely lovely origin story. But a lovely story isn't a reason to spend $40 on a bag of dog food, so this review covers what actually matters: what makes their food different, our top 10 picks across freeze-dried raw, dry kibble and wet food, the real benefits, the honest drawbacks — including the raw-food safety question most brand reviews skip — and five fun recipes you can make at home using their products.

📖 The Story Behind the Name

Marie Moody adopted Stella and Chewy in 1997. Chewy was the one the shelter's vet advised against — he was diagnosed with an autoimmune disease and simply wasn't thriving. When conventional feeding wasn't working, the vet suggested a raw diet built around protein, fruits and vegetables, and Marie began preparing it by hand.

Chewy's recovery was the thing that convinced her. She started making the food commercially in 2003, working out of her Brooklyn brownstone kitchen before moving production to Wisconsin — first a 10,000 square foot plant in Muskego, and eventually the 355,000 square foot facility in Oak Creek the company operates today. The tagline that came out of all this, "only the good stuff," is still on every bag.

🎉 Worth knowing

The brand is named after two rescue dogs, and one of them was a dog a professional recommended against adopting. Whatever you conclude about the food, that's a genuinely nice piece of pet industry history — and it's why the packaging carries two dogs' faces rather than a stock photo.

🔬 What Actually Makes It Different

Plenty of brands claim to be premium. Here's what's specifically different about this one, and what each point means in practice.

1. They pasteurise raw food with pressure, not heat

The central objection to raw pet food is pathogens. Stella & Chewy's adopted high-pressure processing (HPP) in 2007, ahead of most of the industry. HPP subjects sealed food to enormous water pressure, which destroys pathogens like Salmonella and Listeria without cooking it — so the nutritional profile of raw is preserved while the microbial risk drops dramatically. They also state that every batch is tested before release, and hold SQF Level 2 certification.

This matters more than any marketing claim on the front of the bag. It's the single biggest reason we're comfortable recommending their raw products when we'd be more cautious about frozen raw generally.

2. Freeze-drying, not cooking

Freeze-drying removes water at low temperature under vacuum, leaving the protein structure, fats and heat-sensitive nutrients largely intact. The result is shelf-stable food you store in a cupboard rather than a freezer, that rehydrates with warm water in about a minute, and that doesn't need the high-heat extrusion that conventional kibble goes through.

3. Very high meat inclusion

Their freeze-dried raw recipes are typically 95% meat, organs and bone — with the organ and bone content being the important part. Muscle meat alone is not a complete diet for a dog; organs supply vitamin A, iron, copper and B vitamins, and ground bone supplies calcium and phosphorus in roughly the ratio a dog evolved to use.

4. They offer grain-free and grain-inclusive

Their Wild Red kibble comes in both a grain and legume-free version and a Wholesome Grains version, and the SuperBlends line is built around digestible grains, superfoods, bone broth and probiotics. Given the ongoing conversation about grain-free diets (more on that below), having both options available is a genuinely sensible position rather than a marketing hedge.

5. Probiotics in everything

Live probiotics and antioxidants are added across the range. Dogs on antibiotics, dogs recovering from digestive upsets and dogs transitioning between foods all benefit from supported gut flora — and gut health is where a lot of the "my dog's coat got shinier" feedback ultimately comes from.
