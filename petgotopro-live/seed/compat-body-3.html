Bottom dwellers & clean-up crew

7 · Corydoras catfish

Corydoras species
Size: 1–3"Min tank: 10 gal (pygmy) / 20 galTemp: 72–79°FLevel: BottomTemperament: Peaceful, social

The most universally compatible fish in the hobby — the one row on our chart that's green almost the whole way across. They're shoaling animals, so six minimum; a lone cory is a stressed cory. Give them sand or smooth gravel, because sharp substrate wears down their barbels and causes infections.

Important: they are not a cleaning crew. Corydoras need their own sinking food — they will starve while politely hoovering up nothing in a tank where everything is eaten at the surface.

Good with: essentially every peaceful community fish, plus angelfish, gouramis and rams.

Avoid: African and large cichlids, goldfish, crayfish.

8 · Bristlenose pleco

Ancistrus species
Size: 4–6"Min tank: 20–29 galTemp: 73–82°FLevel: BottomTemperament: Peaceful, males territorial

This is the pleco to buy — the common pleco sold beside it reaches 18–24 inches. Bristlenose stay manageable, genuinely eat algae, and are tough enough to live alongside semi-aggressive fish. They need driftwood: the cellulose is a dietary requirement, not decoration. Supplement with algae wafers and courgette.

Good with: nearly everything, including angelfish and (often) African cichlids.

Avoid: other male bristlenose in small tanks, goldfish, crayfish.

9 · Otocinclus

Otocinclus species
Size: 1.5–2"Min tank: 10 galTemp: 72–79°FLevel: Bottom/glassTemperament: Peaceful, timid

Tiny, charming and the best true algae eater for a small tank — but they need a mature, established aquarium with real biofilm and soft green algae to graze on. Put them in a new tank and they starve. Most are still wild-caught and arrive stressed, so buy the plumpest ones you can see and keep six or more.

Good with: all peaceful community fish, shrimp, snails.

Avoid: new tanks, cichlids, anything large enough to eat them.

10 · Kuhli loach

Pangio kuhlii
Size: 3–4"Min tank: 20 galTemp: 75–86°FLevel: BottomTemperament: Peaceful, shy

Little eel-like loaches that are entirely harmless and enormously entertaining once settled. Keep five or more — in smaller numbers they hide permanently and you'll never see them. Sand substrate and caves are essential, and a tight lid, because they explore.

Good with: bettas, tetras, rasboras, corydoras, gouramis, angelfish.

Avoid: large cichlids, crayfish, bare tanks with no hiding places.

11 · Snail-eating loaches (clown, yoyo, skunk)

Chromobotia / Botia species
Size: 5–12"Min tank: 40 gal (clown: 75+)Temp: 75–86°FLevel: BottomTemperament: Boisterous, social

Intelligent, playful and genuinely long-lived — clown loaches can pass 20 years and reach a foot, which the 2-inch juvenile in the shop tank does not suggest. They're excellent fish for a big tank and a terrible impulse buy. They must be kept in groups of five or more.

Good with: barbs, danios, larger tetras, plecos, gouramis — in a large tank.

Avoid: all snails, shrimp, small tanks, being kept singly.

Invertebrates

18 · Cherry shrimp (neocaridina)

Size: 1–1.5"Min tank: 5 galTemp: 65–78°FLevel: Bottom/plantsTemperament: Peaceful, prey

Fascinating, hardworking and cheap to start a colony with. The compatibility question isn't whether fish will kill adult shrimp — most won't — it's whether shrimplets survive to adulthood. In a fish community they usually don't. A dense carpet of java moss changes the odds dramatically.

Never use copper-based medications or many snail treatments: copper is lethal to all shrimp.

Good with: corydoras, otocinclus, bristlenose, snails, small peaceful nano fish.

Avoid: angelfish, all cichlids, fin-nippers, crayfish, goldfish, loaches.

19 · Amano shrimp

Caridina multidentata
Size: 2"Min tank: 10 galTemp: 65–80°FLevel: BottomTemperament: Peaceful

Bigger and tougher than cherry shrimp, and the best algae-eating invertebrate you can buy — they'll clear hair algae that nothing else touches. Because of their size they survive in community tanks where cherries would be eaten. They won't breed in freshwater (larvae need brackish), so the population stays exactly where you put it.

Good with: most peaceful community fish, gouramis, rams, corydoras.

Avoid: cichlids, goldfish, crayfish, large predatory fish.

20 · Snails (nerite, mystery, ramshorn)

Size: 0.5–2"Min tank: 5 galTemp: 65–82°FLevel: All surfacesTemperament: Peaceful

Nerites are the best algae eaters and won't breed in freshwater — they lay harmless white eggs that never hatch. Mystery snails are big, personable and only breed above the waterline, so they're easy to control. Ramshorns multiply to match the food available, which makes them either a free clean-up crew or a nuisance depending on how much you overfeed.

All snails need calcium in the water for shell health, and none of them tolerate copper-based medications.

Good with: nearly all community fish and shrimp.

Avoid: snail-eating loaches, pufferfish, assassin snails, most cichlids, crayfish.

21 · Freshwater crabs

Size: 1–3"Setup: Usually paludariumTemp: 75–82°FLevel: Bottom/landTemperament: Opportunistic

Read the species carefully before buying. Red claw crabs need brackish water and dry land to climb out onto — the most commonly mis-sold invertebrate in the trade. Vampire crabs need a planted paludarium, not an aquarium. Thai micro crabs are the genuine exception: fully aquatic, thumbnail-sized, and safe with nano fish and shrimp.

Most crabs are also escape artists and will catch small or sleeping fish given the chance.

Good with: a species-appropriate setup of their own; micro crabs with nano fish.

Avoid: standard community tanks, small fish, shrimp, anything they can grab.

22 · Crayfish / "freshwater lobsters"

Size: 3–6"Min tank: 20 gal, species onlyTemp: 65–80°FLevel: BottomTemperament: Predatory

Genuinely interesting animals with real personality — and completely unsuited to a community tank. They hunt at night, pulling sleeping fish off plants, and they'll eliminate a shrimp colony and a snail population within weeks. They're also cannibalistic when moulting, so even keeping two together needs plenty of caves.

Good with: a tank of their own, with a secure lid.

Avoid: everything else.

Cichlids & coldwater

15 · African cichlids (mbuna)

Size: 4–8"Min tank: 55 gal (75+ better)Temp: 76–82°FLevel: AllTemperament: Aggressive

Spectacular colours, and effectively their own hobby. They want hard, alkaline water (the opposite of what most community fish prefer), a rock-piled tank, and — counter-intuitively — heavy overstocking, which spreads aggression so no single fish is targeted. Almost nothing else can live with them; bristlenose plecos and some synodontis catfish are the usual exceptions.

Avoid: mixing with any community fish, soft water, understocking, small tanks.

16 · Large American cichlids (oscar, jack dempsey, convict)

Size: 6–14"Min tank: 55–125 gal by speciesTemp: 74–82°FLevel: AllTemperament: Aggressive, territorial

Oscars are famously personable — they recognise their owners and can be hand-fed — and famously messy, needing oversized filtration and big weekly water changes. A single oscar needs 75 gallons minimum. Convicts are the small exception at 5–6 inches, but they breed relentlessly and defend their fry against everything in the tank.

Avoid: any fish small enough to swallow, shrimp, snails, undersized tanks.

17 · Goldfish

Carassius auratus
Size: 6–8" fancy, 12"+ commonMin tank: 20–40 gal fancy; pond for commonsTemp: 65–72°FLevel: AllTemperament: Peaceful but messy

Coldwater fish that belong with other goldfish. Don't mix fancy varieties (fantails, orandas, ryukins) with single-tailed ones (commons, comets, shubunkins) either — the streamlined single-tails are far faster and outcompete the fancies for food, and commons reach a foot or more and truly need a pond.

Good with: other goldfish of the same body type; white cloud minnows in cooler setups.

Avoid: tropical fish, shrimp, small snails, anything needing warm water.
