Two tests, three lists, and the signs that mean call today

✅ Safe Everyday Chews

Bully sticksOne ingredient, digests like meat
Softer bully sticksSeniors, worn or repaired teeth
Beef green tripe sticksSoft, low fat, very smelly
Turkey or duck necksRaw only — never cooked
Chicken feetSoft, crunchy, gone in minutes
Hollow rubber toysStuff and freeze — no hardness risk

⚠️ Risky — Watch Closely

Rawhide, any formSwells in the gut, does not break down
Knotted rawhide bonesNever for a strong chewer
Pig earsFatty; swallowed whole once short
Cow earsFine, but count the calories
Any chew worn to 2 inThe highest-risk moment of all
A chew near another dogSeparate them, every time

🚫 Never, At Any Size

Antlers, horns, hoovesHarder than tooth enamel
Weight-bearing bonesFemur and knuckle break teeth
Any cooked boneSplinters — smoked and baked too
Hard nylon chewsFails the nail test, adds shards
Rocks, sticks, ice cubesFracture and splinter

💚 A Chew Session Going Right

  • Steady, unhurried chewing — not frantic cracking
  • The surface marks and wears down gradually
  • The dog settles with it rather than guarding it
  • You are in the room, the whole time, on a hard floor
  • Normal stools and normal appetite the next morning
  • You trade for something better instead of taking it by hand

⚠️ Call the Vet the Same Day If

  • Repeated retching that brings nothing up
  • Drooling, or refusing water
  • A hunched or praying posture
  • A swollen or painful belly
  • Vomiting that will not stop
  • A tooth gone pink, gray or dark — the pulp is exposed

The 4 Rules That Prevent Almost Every Problem

1

If your thumbnail cannot dent it, it does not come home. Test it in the shop, before it is paid for. A chew that outlasts a determined dog is harder than that dog's teeth, and the upper fourth premolar is the one that breaks.

2

Longer than the muzzle, and gone at the last two inches. A dog worked down to a two-inch stub will try to swallow it whole rather than give it up, and that is exactly the wrong size for an esophagus.

3

Supervise every chew, every single time. Not the next room, not with the television on. This is the step everybody skips and it is the one that matters.

4

Chews are food, so count them. A twelve-inch bully stick is about a third of a 20-pound dog's entire day, which is how a dog gains weight on a diet nobody changed.
