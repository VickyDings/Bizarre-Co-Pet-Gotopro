Pet-Go'to'Pro Quick Care Sheet

Freshwater ShrimpNeocaridina & Caridina

Habitat, equipment and diet on one page — and the four things that actually kill them

🏠 Habitat

Minimum tank5 gallons · 10 is easier
Starting colony10–15 animals, one color line
SubstrateInert gravel or sand (Neocaridina)
PlantingHeavy. Java moss, anubias, ferns
Temperature68–78°F · heater rarely needed
Before stockingCycle fully, then wait 2 more weeks

💧 Water

pH6.5–8.0 · stable beats ideal
GH6–10 dGH — decides molting
KH2–8 dKH
TDS180–300 ppm
Ammonia / nitrite0, always
Water changes10–20% weekly, temp and TDS matched

🧩 Necessities

FilterSponge filter — cannot eat babies
Test kitsMaster kit AND a separate GH/KH kit
DechlorinatorEvery drop of tap water, no exceptions
MineralsGH/KH+ salt if GH tests under 5
Cover for babiesA thick clump of java moss
Nice to haveTDS pen, cholla wood, almond leaves

🍳 Diet

The stapleBiofilm — grazed all day, not fed
Prepared foodRice-grain size per 10 shrimp, every 2 days
Remove uneatenWithin 3 hours
VegetablesBlanched zucchini or spinach, 1–2× a month
BotanicalsOne almond leaf a month
Molted shellsLeave them. They eat them back

✅ A colony that is fine

  • Grazing constantly, out in the open
  • Deep, even color that holds
  • Berried females fanning eggs
  • Clear shed shells appearing regularly
  • Babies at several different sizes
  • Fast, deliberate movement when startled

🚩 Stop and test the water

  • Sitting still in the open, not grazing
  • Swimming frantically along the glass
  • Color draining across the whole colony
  • A white ring behind the head — failed molt
  • Every female dropping eggs at once
  • Deaths 2–3 days after a water change

The four things that kill them — in order

1

Copper. Lethal at 0.01–0.03 ppm, which no standard test kit sees. Fish medications, snail killers and many plant fertilizers all contain it. Never medicate a tank with shrimp in it — move them out first.

2

A chemistry swing. Shrimp tolerate a wide range but not fast movement through it. A large, cold, mismatched water change kills two or three days later, during the next molt, which is why the cause gets missed.

3

Low GH. Shells are built from minerals in the water. Too soft and the molt splits all the way around instead of hinging open — the white ring of death. Test GH; it is not in the master kit.

4

A tank that is too new. Cycled is not the same as ready. Shrimp live on biofilm, and a spotless four-week-old tank has none. Wait two more weeks and let the glass go slightly grubby first.

Molt or death? Hold it up to the light.
Clear and hollow = a shed shellOpaque white or pink = a dead shrimpWhite ring behind the head = a molt going wrong
