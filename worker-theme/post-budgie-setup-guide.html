Budgies are sold as a beginner bird and priced like an impulse buy, and that is exactly why so many of them live short, dull, uncomfortable lives. The bird is not the problem. A budgerigar is a small, clever, intensely social parrot that flies tens of kilometres a day across the Australian interior — and we hand it a round cage, a bowl of seed and a plastic mirror, then wonder why it plucks, screams or dies at four.

This guide is the setup we would give a friend. Cage sizes matched to how many birds you actually have. What a wide cage buys you that a tall one never will. How to tell a cock from a hen without guessing — and the varieties where the usual method flatly does not work. And because it is the question we are asked more than any other: how budgie breeding actually goes, what it costs the hen, and the supplements that stand between a laying female and an emergency vet bill.

Wild-type green budgies. In the Outback they move in flocks of hundreds — which tells you most of what you need to know about keeping one alone.

The test that beats every number above: your budgie should be able to stand in the middle of the cage, open both wings fully, and turn a complete circle without a feather touching anything. Then it should be able to fly — not hop — from one perch to another. If the only way across the cage is a hop, the cage is a holding pen.

Two rules that decide cage shape

Get these wrong and no amount of money fixes it.

Wide beats tall, always

Budgies fly horizontally. They do not hover and they do not climb like a cockatiel or a conure. A 30-inch-tall, 16-inch-wide cage looks generous on a shelf and gives your bird almost nowhere to go — the top third is dead space it will never use.

Given a choice between two cages of the same volume, take the wider one every single time.

Never buy a round cage

Round cages have no corner for a bird to retreat into, which keeps a prey animal in low-level stress permanently. The bars also converge at the top into a narrowing wedge that can trap a head, a wing or a foot.

They were designed to look like a birdcage in a painting. They are a genuinely poor place to live.

⚠️ Bar spacing is the one measurement you cannot compromise on

A budgie's skull is narrower than its shoulders. With bars wider than ½ inch it can push its head through and then physically cannot pull it back — the feathers lie the wrong way. Birds die of this in minutes, and it happens most often overnight when nobody is watching.

Measure between the bars before you buy. ½ inch / 12 mm maximum. Anything sold for cockatiels, conures or "small parrots" is usually ⅝–¾ inch and is not safe for a budgie.

Fun fact

A wild budgie flock can cover tens of kilometres in a day chasing rain across the Australian interior. Flocks of tens of thousands have been recorded moving through the Outback after a downpour — one of the largest parrot gatherings on earth.

Where you put the cage matters as much as the cage

Against a wall, not floating in the middle of a room — a cage with open space on all four sides leaves a prey animal with nothing at its back. At roughly chest height, so you are not looming over your bird every time you walk past. In a room the family actually uses, because a budgie alone in a spare bedroom is a budgie in solitary confinement.

And never, under any circumstances, in the kitchen. That is not about mess.

Perches: bin the ones that came in the box

Every cage on the market ships with one or two smooth wooden dowels of identical diameter. They are there because they are cheap to mould, and they are the single most common cause of foot problems in pet budgies.

A bird standing on the same diameter in the same grip all day gets constant pressure on the same points of the foot. That produces flat, shiny, reddened soles, then pressure sores, then bumblefoot — a deep infection that needs veterinary treatment and often never fully resolves.

Use these

  • Natural hardwood branches, bark on
  • Varied diameters, ⅜–¾ in across the cage
  • Irregular, slightly bent shapes
  • Apple, willow, manzanita, eucalyptus, dogwood
  • One rope perch for grip variety
  • A flat platform perch for resting older birds

Bin these

  • The smooth dowels that came with the cage
  • Sandpaper perch covers — they abrade, not trim
  • Plastic perches of any kind
  • Anything treated, painted or varnished
  • Cherry, plum, apricot, oak, yew — toxic
  • Perches directly above food or water bowls

Three or four perches at different heights and diameters is the target. Put the highest one where the bird can survey the room — budgies sleep high and feel safest there — and leave the flight path between perches genuinely clear.

Diet: the seed myth is what kills most pet budgies

An all-seed diet is the leading cause of premature death in captive budgerigars, and it is what nearly every new owner is sold on day one. Seed is high in fat, low in calcium, low in vitamin A, and almost entirely missing iodine. A budgie left on it develops obesity, then fatty liver disease, then — often — a thyroid goitre that makes it click and wheeze when it breathes.

The birds also do something maddening: offered a full bowl of mixed seed, a budgie will eat only its two favourite seeds and leave the rest. The bowl looks full. The bird is starving inside it.

ComponentShare of daily intakeNotes
Formulated pellets60–70%The nutritional backbone. Buy a fine or small-parrot grind — budgie beaks cannot manage cockatiel-sized pellets.
Fresh vegetables & greens20–30%Kale, romaine, bell pepper, broccoli, carrot tops, peas, cooked sweet potato, dandelion greens.

FruitSmall amountsApple (no seeds), berries, melon. High sugar — a few times a week, not daily.

Converting a seed addict takes weeks, not days. Never go cold turkey — a budgie will starve rather than eat something it does not recognise as food. Mix a small amount of pellets into the seed and increase the ratio over three to four weeks, and weigh your bird on a gram scale twice a week through the switch. A drop of more than 10% means back off and slow down.

⚠️ Never feed a budgie these

Avocado — persin is lethal to birds, often within hours. Chocolate and caffeine. Onion and garlic. Apple seeds, cherry and apricot pits, rhubarb leaves. Salty, fried or processed human food. Alcohol. Uncooked beans.

And the one nobody expects: never heat non-stick cookware in a home with a bird. PTFE fumes from pans, air fryers, self-cleaning ovens, some space heaters and even certain hair tools kill budgies within thirty minutes. There is no treatment and no warning smell.

Fun fact

Budgies see in the ultraviolet range. Their yellow face and crown feathers fluoresce under UV light, and studies show budgies prefer mates whose crowns glow more brightly. To another budgie, your bird is wearing colours you physically cannot perceive.

Sexing a budgie: reading the cere, and when it lies to you

The cere is the fleshy band of skin above the beak where the nostrils sit. In most budgie varieties it is a reliable sex indicator in adults — and completely unreliable in chicks, in certain colour mutations, and in hens whose hormones are doing something interesting.

Age / stateCock (male)Hen (female)
Chick, under ~8 weeksUniform pinkish-purple, smoothPale pink to white, often with visible whitish rings around the nostrils
Juvenile, 2–4 monthsPink deepening toward lilac or lavenderChalky white, very pale blue, or pale pink — and those nostril rings persist
Adult, 8–12 months onSolid royal to deep blue, smooth and evenChalky white to pale blue when out of condition
Adult hen in breeding conditionNo change — stays blueTan, then deep crusty brown, visibly raised and rough

The varieties where the cere never tells you anything

This is the part that catches people out, and it is why so many "he" budgies suddenly lay eggs. In several mutations, the male's cere never turns blue — it stays pink or purple for life, which looks female to everyone who has only read the standard chart.

  • Albino and lutino (the "ino" family) — cocks keep a pink to purple cere permanently
  • Dark-eyed clear
  • Recessive pied — and many Danish pied cocks
  • Fallow
  • Any bird carrying a heavy pied pattern across the face

In those varieties the only certain answer is a DNA sex test. You post a freshly plucked chest feather or a drop of blood on a card to a lab and get a result back in a few days, usually for $20–$30. If you are buying a bird specifically to pair it, and it is any of the varieties above, test it. Discovering the truth via an unexpected clutch of eggs is the expensive way.

Behaviour, as a second opinion

Not proof on its own, but useful alongside the cere.

Usually a cock

  • Long, complex, rambling warble — cocks are the singers and the talkers
  • Head-bobbing displays, often at a mirror, a toy or you
  • Tapping the beak against a perch
  • Regurgitating for a favourite person or object
  • Generally more placid in the hand

Usually a hen

  • Chews everything — paper, wood, cage covers, your furniture
  • Louder single squawks rather than sustained warbling
  • Territorial about the cage, especially near a favoured corner
  • More likely to bite, and to mean it
  • Investigates dark enclosed spaces — the nest-site instinct
Fun fact

A budgie's warble is a learned social signature, not a fixed call. Flock-mates converge on a shared "contact call" over time, and a budgie moved into a new flock will gradually rewrite its own call to match the group. Your bird learns your household's soundtrack the same way — which is exactly why they pick up microwaves, phone alerts and your laugh.

One, two or a flock?

A single budgie can be very happy if you are genuinely present — several hours of interaction a day, every day, for the next decade. That bird will bond hard to you, talk more, and tame faster, because you are its entire flock. Miss that commitment and you have a socially starved animal that screams, plucks or simply shuts down.

Two budgies keep each other occupied all day and are far more forgiving of a normal working life. They will be less interested in you — that is the honest trade — but both birds are better off. Two hens can work but squabble over territory; two cocks are usually the most peaceful combination; a cock and hen will breed given any excuse at all, which is a decision, not an accident (see below).

Three or more and you have flock behaviour: constant chatter, real social structure, and a genuinely good life for the birds — provided the cage scales with the number, per the table at the top.

💚 Quarantine every new bird for 30–45 days

A separate cage in a different room, with its own food bowls, and you deal with the new bird last every single day so you are not carrying anything back to the established one. Psittacine beak and feather disease, polyomavirus, air-sac mites and chlamydia all incubate silently, and a new budgie that looks perfect can still be shedding.

A vet check during quarantine — a basic exam plus a faecal — is money well spent before two birds ever share air.

Breeding budgies: the honest version

Budgies breed readily. That is the problem. Give a cock and a hen a dark enclosed space, long daylight and rich food, and they will produce eggs whether or not anybody involved is ready — and every part of the cost lands on the hen.

Laying strips calcium out of her skeleton. Incubating keeps her on the nest for three weeks. Feeding a clutch of six can drop her body weight by a quarter. Do it badly, or too often, or too young, and you get egg binding, a prolapse, chronic laying, or a dead hen. This is the section people skip and then message us about at 11pm.

⚠️ Do not breed if any of these are true

The hen is under 12 months old. Sexual maturity arrives around six months; physical readiness does not. Under a year, the pelvis has not finished widening and egg binding risk climbs sharply. Eighteen months is the number experienced breeders actually use.

Either bird is unwell, underweight, or on an all-seed diet. You do not have an avian vet you can reach the same day. You have not found homes for six to eight birds. You do not know both birds' genetic background — pairing two birds carrying the same lethal or deforming factors is how you get chicks that do not survive weaning.

Before you put the box up

1
Confirm both sexes properly Cere colour for standard varieties; DNA test for any ino, pied, fallow or dark-eyed clear. Two hens given a nest box will both lay infertile eggs and fight over the same hollow.
2
Get both birds onto a proper diet at least 6–8 weeks ahead You cannot fix a calcium deficit once the hen is already laying. Pellets, daily greens, cuttlebone and a mineral block need to be routine before the box goes anywhere near the cage.
3
Let the pair choose each other A bonded pair preen each other, feed each other and sleep touching. Two birds shoved together that have not bonded will produce infertile clutches, and sometimes a cock that injures the hen.
4
Have the emergency plan written down before you need it Your avian vet's number and out-of-hours arrangement, on the fridge. A small hospital cage or carrier with a heat source you can set up in five minutes. Egg binding is measured in hours.

The nest box

Budgies are cavity nesters. In the wild they use hollows in eucalyptus trunks, and they bring in no nesting material at all — the eggs sit directly on the wood. A box stuffed with hay or shredded paper is a box a budgie has to clear out before it can start.

What actually happens, week by week

  1. Days 1–7 — conditioning. Box goes up. Extend daylight to 12–14 hours, start egg food and sprouted seed, make sure cuttlebone and a mineral block are in place. The hen begins inspecting the box and chewing at the entrance; the cock feeds her and displays.
  2. Days 7–14 — mating and the first egg. The hen's cere goes deep crusty brown. Expect the first egg roughly 8–10 days after the first successful mating. She will lay one egg every other day, typically 4–8 in total.
  3. Days 14–32 — incubation. The hen sits alone and the cock feeds her at the hole; she leaves only briefly. Incubation is about 18 days from each egg, so because the eggs were laid on alternate days, they hatch on alternate days too. Leave the box alone apart from a quick daily check.
  4. Hatching — staggered, and it looks alarming. The oldest chick can be four times the size of the youngest. That is normal budgie biology, not a problem to fix. Eyes open around day 7; the first pin feathers show around day 10.
  5. Days 7–10 — ring, if you are ringing. Closed leg rings only go on in this narrow window, before the foot is too big. After day 10 it is too late.
  6. Weeks 3–5 — the messy phase. Chicks are feathering and the box gets filthy. Clean the concave carefully — caked droppings on a chick's feet cause deformity — and keep soft food coming twice daily.
  7. Days 30–35 — fledging. Chicks leave the box. They fly badly and crash into things, so remove or lower high perches and cover windows and mirrors for the first week.
  8. Weeks 6–8 — weaning. The cock does most of the feeding now. Put millet spray and shallow dishes of soft food at floor level where the fledglings can reach. They are independent around eight weeks — not before.
  9. The moment the last chick weans — take the box down. Leave it up and the hen goes straight into a second clutch while still feeding the first. Two clutches a year is the ceiling. Three is how hens die.

💚 Bringing a hen out of condition, on purpose

After the box comes down: cut daylight back to 8–10 hours, stop all soft and egg food, remove anything she can treat as a nest hollow (huts, tents, cardboard, dark corners), and rearrange the cage furniture so it stops feeling like a settled territory. Keep cuttlebone available — she is still rebuilding.

Give her a minimum of six months before even considering another clutch, and check her weight is back to baseline first.

Fun fact

Budgie hens do not wait for a full clutch before they start sitting — incubation begins with the first egg. That is why the chicks hatch in a staircase of sizes. In a drought year, the smallest chicks simply do not make it, and the oldest two fledge strong. It is brutal, and it is precisely what let budgerigars colonise the driest continent on earth.

Breeding supplements and the extra care a laying hen needs

The single most useful thing to understand: every egg is made largely of the hen's own calcium reserves, and she can lay eight of them in a fortnight. A hen who starts a clutch already short on calcium runs out mid-clutch, her uterus loses the muscle tone to push, and the egg stops. That is egg binding, and it kills within a day if untreated.

⚠️ Egg binding — recognise it in seconds, act in minutes

The signs: sitting fluffed on the cage floor, straining or pumping the tail, a wide-legged stance, a visibly swollen or firm abdomen, not perching, sudden weakness in one or both legs (the egg presses on the sciatic nerve), laboured breathing.

What to do: avian vet, same day, no waiting to see. On the way, keep her warm — 85°F / 29°C — and humid; heat and humidity alone sometimes let a hen pass the egg. Never try to manipulate or break the egg yourself. A broken egg inside a hen means shell fragments, infection and usually death.

Also call the vet for: a prolapse (pink or red tissue protruding from the vent), a hen laying clutch after clutch with no nest box, or a chick with legs splaying sideways instead of underneath it.

Health: what to watch, and what is an emergency

Budgies are prey animals and hide illness for as long as they physically can. By the time a budgie looks sick to a casual glance, it has usually been unwell for days. This is why the gram scale earns its place — weight moves before behaviour does.

💚 A healthy budgie

  • Bright, clear, fully open eyes
  • Smooth dry cere, no crust or discharge
  • Sleek feathers held close to the body
  • Active, chirping, chewing, climbing
  • Hulled husks in the seed dish — not whole seed
  • Clean vent, firm well-formed droppings
  • Steady weight, week to week

⚠️ Call an avian vet today

  • Tail bobbing with every breath — respiratory distress
  • Sitting fluffed on the cage floor
  • Clicking, wheezing or open-mouth breathing
  • Whole unhulled seed left on the floor — not eating
  • Droppings runny or discoloured beyond one day
  • Weight down more than 10%
  • Crusty growths on cere, beak or legs — scaly face mites

Find an avian vet before you need one. A general small-animal practice often cannot treat birds, and the search takes longer than an emergency gives you. Save the number now.

A budgie will act completely normal until the day it cannot. The owners who catch things early are not more observant — they are the ones with a scale and a notebook.
What every avian vet wishes owners knew

Your first two weeks with a new budgie

  1. Days 1–3 — do nothing. Cage set up, food and water in, and leave the bird alone. Sit in the room and read aloud or talk quietly. No hands in the cage beyond what feeding requires.
  2. Days 4–6 — become furniture. Rest a hand on the outside of the cage while you talk. When the bird stops freezing every time you move, you have progress.
  3. Days 7–9 — hand inside, still. Open the door, rest your hand just inside, and do not reach. Hold still for five minutes at a time. Millet in your fingers, offered without pursuit.
  4. Days 10–12 — millet from the hand. When the bird takes millet from your fingers without retreating, you have the relationship. Do not rush to touch it.
  5. Days 13–14 — step up. Press a finger gently against the lower chest, just above the legs. The bird steps up because balance says to. Never grab, and never chase a budgie around a cage — you will undo two weeks in ten seconds.

More fun facts to win an argument with

Fun fact

A budgie named Puck holds the Guinness World Record for the largest vocabulary of any bird — 1,728 words. Budgies do not have vocal cords; they shape sound with the syrinx and the tongue, which is why their speech has that distinctive compressed, gravelly quality.

Fun fact

The scientific name Melopsittacus undulatus means "melodious parrot with wavy markings". Those black scalloped bars across the wings and head are how you age a young bird: chicks have barring right down to the cere, and it clears from the forehead at the first moult around 12–16 weeks. A budgie still showing forehead bars is under four months old.

Fun fact

Budgies are contagious yawners — the only non-mammal in which contagious yawning has been demonstrated. It is considered a marker of basic empathy, and it puts budgerigars in a very small club alongside humans, dogs, chimps and rats.

Fun fact

Every budgie on earth descends from the wild Australian green form. The first blue mutation appeared in captivity in 1878, and at the peak of the 1920s breeding boom a single pair of blue budgies sold for the price of a house.

Frequently asked questions

What cage size do I need for two budgies?

Minimum 30 × 18 × 18 inches (76 × 46 × 46 cm), but buy 39 inches wide if you can. Two birds need enough space to move away from each other — in a cramped cage, ordinary bickering escalates into real aggression. Bar spacing must be ½ inch or less whatever the size.

How can I tell if my budgie is male or female?

Look at the cere, the fleshy band above the beak. In adults over about eight months, a cock's cere is solid blue and a hen's is chalky white, pale blue, or crusty brown when she is in breeding condition. Chicks of both sexes have pale pink ceres, so you cannot sex a young bird reliably. Albino, lutino, recessive pied, dark-eyed clear and fallow cocks keep a pink cere for life — those need a DNA test.

How old should a budgie be before breeding?

At least 12 months, and 18 months is safer for the hen. Budgies can reproduce from around six months, but a young hen's body is not ready — egg binding risk is far higher and young parents often abandon or fail to feed chicks. Cocks should also be at least a year old.

How many eggs do budgies lay and how long until they hatch?

Typically 4–8 eggs, one laid every other day. Incubation is about 18 days per egg, and because the hen starts sitting on the first egg, the chicks hatch on alternate days too — so a clutch contains chicks of dramatically different sizes. That is normal.

Do budgies need nesting material in the box?

No. Budgies are cavity nesters and lay directly on the wooden floor. What the box does need is a carved concave — a shallow hollow — in the base. On a flat floor, eggs roll into corners and chicks develop splayed leg, a permanent hip deformity.

What supplements does a breeding budgie need?

Calcium with vitamin D3 is the critical one — start three to four weeks before pairing, not after the first egg. Alongside it: cuttlebone always available, egg or soft food from two weeks before pairing through weaning, sprouted seed, an iodine block, and a full-spectrum lamp so the bird can actually use the calcium. Never grit.

How many clutches a year is safe?

Two, maximum, with at least six months between them. Take the nest box down the moment the last chick weans — leave it up and the hen will start a second clutch while still feeding the first, which is how hens end up depleted, egg-bound or dead.

Can a single budgie be happy on its own?

Yes, but only if you are genuinely its flock — several hours of real interaction every day for the bird's whole life. If you work full-time or travel, get two. A lone, under-stimulated budgie develops screaming, feather plucking and stereotypies, and those are very hard to reverse once established.

Why is my budgie's cere changing colour?

In a hen, brown means she has come into breeding condition — normal, though worth managing if there is no nest box and she is laying anyway. In a cock, a cere turning from blue to brown is not normal and is a classic sign of a testicular tumour. Book an avian vet.
